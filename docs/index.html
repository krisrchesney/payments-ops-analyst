Payments Ops Analyst

A Spark pipeline on Databricks turns synthetic card authorizations into operational reports. A Claude agent then explains why the numbers moved, using the reports for what happened and searchable runbooks and change logs for why.

What it does

The question below names neither electronics nor fraud, so the agent has to work out where to look.

Question

Approval rates looked off during the week of September 21. Find what moved, explain why it happened, cite any document you relied on, and back it up with numbers from the gold tables. Tell me anything you could not confirm.

What moved, from the reportsSuspected-fraud declines on online electronics went from about 8 a day to 666, 678 and 650 on Sep 22 to 24, then back to 10. Ecommerce approval fell from 91.66% to 88.89%. Other channels stayed within 0.2 points.
Why, from the documentsThe fraud rule change log shows FR-2041 tightening the online electronics threshold at 00:00 UTC on Sep 22, and its rollback at 00:00 UTC on Sep 25. The estimated effect was about 1.5 times normal.

It also listed what it could not confirm. For example, the reports have no fraud-outcome labels, so it could not say the declined customers were good ones.

Every figure was then checked with SQL. All matched except one rounding: it said "about 80 times" where the figures give about 86. It also said it could not show the rise was spread across banks when the reports could have shown that. Read the full answer and the check.

Where the data sits in a card payment

The merchant's bank (the acquirer) sends an authorization request through a card network's switch to the cardholder's bank (the issuer). An approve or decline comes back the same way in near real time. Clearing and settlement move the money later.

Cardholdercard_token, is_tokenized, channel
Merchantmerchant_id, mcc, region
AcquirerNot modeled
Network and switchNot modeled as a step. latency_ms is one total.
Issuerissuer_id, and its decision in response_code

Dashed boxes are not in this data, and neither are clearing and settlement. Chargebacks are included and link back to the approved auth_id.

The pipeline

A Spark Declarative Pipeline (Lakeflow) in PySpark, serverless, deployed from git with a Databricks bundle into Unity Catalog.

Three hidden incidents

The generator hides three incidents in the data, with a separate answer key. The test: can the reports pin down when, where and why? Each one was first traced by hand from the reports, then checked against the key.

IncidentWhat movedCause
Sep 12, one bankCode 91 (issuer unavailable): 178 declines that day at this bank, against 8 to 14 on the days around it.The issuer stopped answering.
Sep 17, UK, 09:00 to 11:00 UTCHourly p95 latency of 2,016 to 2,084 ms, against about 348 ms typical. Approvals were unaffected.Slow authorizations in the UK.
Sep 22 to 24, online electronicsCode 59 (suspected fraud) on online electronics: 666, 678 and 650 a day, against about 8. In-store electronics were unaffected.A fraud rule tightened on online electronics.

Averages hide short or narrow problems. The bank outage did not show at the day and region level. It appeared only after filtering to code 91 and ranking by bank and day. The investigation also showed the reports had no merchant category view, so a fifth gold table was added.

The agent and its tools

An MCP server gives Claude six read-only tools. Five query the exported gold tables, using in-memory DuckDB with file and network access off and settings locked. The sixth, search_runbooks, searches the documents. Seventeen automated tests cover the server without needing Databricks.

  • Asked in plain English, Claude used the tools to find all three incidents, and it said what the data could not prove.
  • It drafted a daily ops note for an incident day, with cause, numbers, caveats and next steps. All 10 figures checked out, and it flagged on its own that the incident had skewed its baseline.

Searching the documents (RAG)

The reports can show that declines jumped. They can't show that someone changed a fraud rule that morning. That reason is written in a document, so the agent needs a way to find it. Keyword search wouldn't be enough, because someone asking about declined electronics orders won't type "FR-2041."

StepIn this project
Documents10 synthetic files: fraud rule and platform change logs, issuer notices, three runbooks, two guides and two past incidents. The three real causes sit among unrelated changes, and no document mentions the incident IDs.
SplitThe pipeline cuts them at each heading into a docs_chunks table of 52 sections.
Embed and indexA Databricks Vector Search Delta Sync index, with managed embeddings from databricks-gte-large-en.
RetrieveThe read-only search_runbooks tool returns the closest sections, with the file and heading to cite.
AnswerClaude treats a matching document as a lead and checks that its timing and scope fit the numbers.

On the live index, 6 of 6 plain-English questions found the expected document in the top 3, and the document behind each incident ranked first or second. I wrote these questions knowing the answers, and the scores sit close together (one correct result won 0.620 to 0.617), so this is a smoke test, not a full evaluation.

Row counts can pass while the content is wrong. The first section table had exactly the right 52 rows, yet every row held its whole document. A check of the actual text caught it. The fix came with a data quality rule that now fails the pipeline if it happens again.
