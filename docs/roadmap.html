Scope

The goal is to help a payment operations team answer one question faster: what changed, where, and why?

In scope
  • Daily reports on approvals, declines, response times and disputes
  • An AI assistant that explains changes from those reports and from searchable documents
  • A drafted daily note for the operations manager
  • Testing the assistant against known answers
  • Automatic flags for unusual changes, with an alert and a dashboard
Out of scope
  • Real card data: everything here is simulated
  • Fraud scoring, or changing whether a payment is approved
  • The acquirer, the network as a separate step, and clearing and settlement

Oldest first.

  1. October 27

    Product plan

    A requirements document with users, success metrics and risks, then epics and user stories with acceptance criteria.

    Why
    To settle who this is for and how success would be judged before building anything.
    Value
    Later results could be measured against targets written in advance.
  2. October 27

    Simulated data with planted problems

    2 million card authorizations and 7,497 chargebacks across 800 merchants and 40 banks, with three planted problems and a separate answer key.

    Why
    Real card data can't be used, and testing needs problems whose true cause is known.
    Value
    Every later result can be checked against an answer instead of judged by feel.
  3. October 31

    Reporting pipeline

    A Spark pipeline on Databricks that turns raw files into daily reports, deployed from code, with data quality rules.

    Why
    Analysts can only explain a change if they trust the numbers underneath it.
    Value
    The quality rules were proven: a test file's 10 planted bad rows and 2 duplicates were removed, and its 5 good rows were kept.
  4. October 31

    Finding the problems by hand

    All three planted problems found from the reports, then confirmed against the answer key.

    Why
    To learn what an analyst actually has to do before asking an AI to do it.
    Value
    It exposed a gap in the reports, which led to a new one by merchant category. It also showed that averages hide short or narrow problems.
  5. October 31 to November 1

    AI assistant with read-only tools

    Claude connected to the reports through a Model Context Protocol server, with tools that can look things up but can't change anything.

    Why
    Slicing reports by region, bank, category and channel is the slow part of an investigation.
    Value
    Anyone can ask in plain English and get an answer that names the numbers it used, with no risk to the data.
  6. November 1

    Drafted daily note

    The assistant writes a short note for the operations manager, covering the cause, numbers, caveats and next steps.

    Why
    Managers need a short summary each day, and it's built from the same analysis.
    Value
    It gives the manager a draft to edit. All 10 figures in the sample matched the data.
  7. November 1 to 2

    Document search

    Ten simulated change logs, notices and how-to guides, split into 52 sections and searchable by meaning (RAG).

    Why
    Reports show what changed, but the reason is usually written down somewhere else.
    Value
    Answers can cite the document behind a cause. In a live check, the expected document was in the top 3 results for 6 of 6 questions.
  8. November 1 to 2

    Public release

    The code, tests, sample answers and this site published under an open license, with the tests running automatically on GitHub.

    Why
    So anyone reviewing this project can see the actual work.
    Value
    Anyone can read the code and run the tests without an account.
  9. November 2

    Formal evaluation

    12 fresh runs graded against rules written first, with every figure rechecked. 8 of 12 passed.

    Why
    One good answer doesn't show much. Repeating each question under fixed rules shows how often the assistant gets it right.
    Value
    It shows what the assistant does well and where it needs checking. It found the true cause in all 9 runs that pointed to a problem, but 2 of those answers had a wrong figure, and only 1 of 3 no-hint runs found all three problems.
  10. November 2

    Release notes

    A list of what shipped in each release, what was checked, and its known limits.

    Why
    People using a product need to know what changed and when.
    Value
    It gives one plain record of what was delivered, release by release.
  11. November 2

    One-page product brief

    The customer, the problem, the offer, and a pricing model with its reasoning.

    Why
    To work out who would buy this and how it could be priced.
    Value
    It puts the business case on one page, and it says that the assumptions haven't been tested with real customers.
  12. November 3 to 4

    Hourly data feed

    New simulated payments are written one hour at a time into the landing volume, and the pipeline picks them up, within free-tier limits. A one-hour run on November 3 added exactly the 2,976 rows of that hour. One run on November 4 wrote the other 335 hours of a 14-day test period, and the pipeline picked up 999,936 rows in all. The schedule is paused, because every run starts a pipeline update of about 10 minutes, and hourly runs would use a lot of free-tier compute.

    Why
    Detection needs data that keeps arriving, and the project's data was one finished month.
    Value
    New problems can be planted in data the detector has never seen, which is how the independent test was run. It has never run on its schedule.
  13. November 3 to 8

    Anomaly detection table

    Written, explained thresholds that flag approval drops, decline spikes and slow response times against a normal baseline. All three planted problems were flagged on the first data. A separate test on four new planted problems caught 2 of the 3 it should catch, missed 1 by 2.5 declines, missed the one it was meant to miss, and raised no false alarms.

    Why
    The bank outage barely moved overall approvals, and the UK slowdown didn't move them at all, so a person watching the headline number would miss both.
    Value
    An analyst knows where to look first. The test showed the checks behave as their arithmetic predicts, and showed what a 25-decline minimum costs: a real problem just under it goes unflagged. Four incidents is a small test, in the same simulator.

This sprint

Sprint 6 runs from November 2 to 9. The goal is automatic detection. The detector and its test are done. What's left is making someone aware when it flags something, because today a person still has to look.

  1. Not started

    An alert when something is flagged, and a dashboard showing approvals, decline reasons, response times and open flags.

    Why
    The project has no graphs or alerts today, so someone has to go looking for problems.
    Value
    The detector would flag a problem, the dashboard would show it, and the assistant would explain why it happened.
